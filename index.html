Your first class in three steps

  1. Come watch or try a class. No appointment and no cost. Talk to an instructor before or after.
  2. Register with Parks & Rec. Pay the monthly rate through Petaluma Parks & Recreation (search "Judo"). Family discounts available.
  3. Wear athletic clothes. Nothing with zippers or buttons. We'll help you get a gi when you're ready.

What we teach

The Kodokan curriculum, the same one taught in Tokyo. Everyone starts by learning to fall.

  1. Ukemi — falling safely

    The first skill every student learns, and the one they use most. Nobody is thrown before they can land.

  2. Throws & groundwork

    Nage-waza — hip throws, foot sweeps, shoulder throws — and katame-waza: pins, turnovers, escapes, and for teens and adults, chokes and arm-locks.

  3. Randori, kata & etiquette

    Free practice with a partner, the traditional forms, the Japanese terminology, and the manners that go with all of it.

Full curriculum and promotion

Maximum efficiency. Mutual welfare and benefit.

Judo's two guiding principles, from its founder Jigoro Kano. It uses leverage, balance and your opponent's own strength — and the principles are as much about how you live as how you throw.
