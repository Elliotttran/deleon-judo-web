Classes & Schedule

Three evenings a week at Herzog Hall. Beginners welcome on Tuesdays and Thursdays.

DayTimeWho

Location: Herzog Hall, Sonoma-Marin Fairgrounds, 175 Fairgrounds Drive, Petaluma, CA 94952. Please arrive 10 minutes early so class can start on time.

What each class looks like

Tuesday & Thursday, 5:30 pm

All students · Ages 6+

Our main class. Kids, teens, and adults train together under multiple instructors. New students focus on ukemi (falling safely), basic grips, and their first throws. Everyone warms up, drills, and practices together; students are paired by size and experience.

Monday, 5:30 pm

Experienced students

A faster-paced class for students who have the fundamentals down. More randori (free practice), competition preparation, and refinement of technique. Instructors will let you know when you're ready to join.

Tuesday & Thursday, 7:00 pm

By appointment only

Focused sessions arranged directly with the instructors — extra mat time, private instruction, or tournament preparation. Contact Sensei Kaku or Sensei Tillman to set one up.

What we teach

The Kodokan curriculum, taught in the order it was meant to be learned. Every technique is called by its Japanese name — the same names used in every dojo in the world.

First — always

Ukemi 受身 · Falling safely

Back, side, and forward rolling breakfalls. No student is thrown before they can land. It is the skill our students use most often, and the one that carries furthest off the mat.

Nage-waza

Throwing techniques

Hip throws (o-goshi), foot sweeps (de-ashi-barai), shoulder throws (seoi-nage), leg reaps (o-soto-gari), and sacrifice throws. Each is broken down into grip, off-balancing (kuzushi), entry (tsukuri), and execution (kake).

Katame-waza

Groundwork

Pins (osaekomi-waza) such as kesa-gatame and kami-shiho-gatame, plus turnovers, escapes, and transitions from a throw to the ground. Chokes (shime-waza) and arm-locks (kansetsu-waza) are taught to teens and adults only.

Randori

Free practice

Live practice with a resisting partner — the heart of judo training. Introduced gradually, at a pace matched to the student, and always paired by size and experience.

Kata

Forms

Prearranged sequences that preserve the principles behind the techniques. Students begin Nage-no-Kata as they approach brown belt; kata is required for dan promotion.

Reigi & terminology

Etiquette and language

Bowing on and off the mat, care for your partner, keeping your gi and the dojo in order — and the Japanese vocabulary for every technique, command, and score. Terminology is part of every promotion test.

Competition is optional, never required. Students who want to compete are prepared for regional tournaments in Northern California; students who never compete progress through the same curriculum.

How you progress

Rank is earned on the mat over time — it is never bought, and there is no fixed timetable.

StageWhat you're working on
First weeksUkemi, dojo etiquette, basic grips and movement, your first throw and first pin.
Colored belts (kyu)A widening set of throws and groundwork, Japanese terminology, and steadily more randori. Promotions come as the technique, knowledge, and mat time are there.
Brown beltRefinement rather than accumulation — combinations, counters, and the beginning of kata. Senior students start helping teach.
Black belt (dan)Demonstrated technique across the full curriculum, kata, and years of consistent practice. Ranks are examined through our yudanshakai and certified by the USJF — recognized nationally.

Promotion follows the standards of the Central Coast Judo Association (CENCO), our USJF yudanshakai — whose Board of Examiners is chaired by our own Sensei Kaku. Ask Sensei Kaku or Sensei Tillman what you're working toward; they'll tell you.

Registration & fees

DeLeon Judo Club does not take payments directly.

  1. Visit or try a class first

    Come watch any Tuesday or Thursday 5:30 pm class, or jump in for a trial. No commitment needed.

  2. Register with Petaluma Parks & Recreation

    Go to the online catalog, search for "Judo," and sign up. Classes are billed at a monthly rate. Family discounts are available when more than one family member enrolls.

  3. Get a gi when you're ready

    Wear athletic clothes to your first classes. Once you decide to continue, you'll need a judo gi — instructors can point you to affordable options.

Register via Petaluma Parks & Rec

What to bring

  • Clothing: a judo gi if you have one; otherwise sweatpants and a t-shirt (no zippers, buttons, or metal).
  • Water bottle.
  • Flip-flops or slides to wear between the door and the mat — shoes are never worn on the tatami.
  • Trimmed fingernails and toenails, no jewelry, long hair tied back.

Beginners start any week

Your first class is just showing up.

Tuesday or Thursday, 5:30 pm. Sweatpants and a t-shirt are all you need — we'll take care of the rest.
