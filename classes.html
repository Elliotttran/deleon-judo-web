What we teach

The Kodokan curriculum, in the order it was meant to be learned. Every technique goes by its Japanese name, the same in every dojo in the world.

Ukemi 受身

Falling safely, always first

Back, side and forward rolling breakfalls. Nobody is thrown before they can land. It's the skill students use most, and the one that carries furthest off the mat.

Nage-waza 投技

Throwing techniques

Hip throws (o-goshi), foot sweeps (de-ashi-barai), shoulder throws (seoi-nage), leg reaps (o-soto-gari) and sacrifice throws, each broken into grip, kuzushi, tsukuri and kake.

Katame-waza 固技

Groundwork

Pins such as kesa-gatame and kami-shiho-gatame, plus turnovers, escapes and the move from a throw to the ground. Chokes and arm-locks are taught to teens and adults only.

Randori 乱取

Free practice

Live practice with a resisting partner, the heart of judo training. Introduced gradually, at a pace matched to the student, and always paired by size and experience.

Kata 形

Forms

Prearranged sequences that keep the principles behind the techniques. Students begin Nage-no-Kata as they approach brown belt; kata is required for dan promotion.

Reigi 礼儀

Etiquette and language

Bowing on and off the mat, care for your partner, keeping your gi and the dojo in order, and the Japanese for every technique, command and score. Terminology is part of every promotion test.

Competition is optional, never required. Students who want to compete are prepared for regional tournaments in Northern California. Students who never compete progress through the same curriculum.

Rank is earned on the mat.

It's never bought, and there's no fixed timetable. Promotion follows the standards of CENCO, our USJF yudanshakai, whose Board of Examiners is chaired by our own Sensei Kaku.

About CENCO
  1. First weeks

    Ukemi, dojo etiquette, basic grips and movement, your first throw and first pin.

  2. Coloured belts (kyu)

    A widening set of throws and groundwork, Japanese terminology and steadily more randori. Promotions come when the technique, knowledge and mat time are there.

  3. Brown belt

    Refinement rather than accumulation: combinations, counters and the start of kata. Senior students begin helping teach.

  4. Black belt (dan)

    Technique across the full curriculum, kata and years of steady practice. Examined through our yudanshakai and certified by the USJF, so it's recognised nationally.

Registration & fees

We don't take payments directly. Classes are billed monthly through Petaluma Parks & Recreation, with family discounts.

Getting started

  1. Visit or try a class first. Watch any Tuesday or Thursday 5:30 pm class, or jump in for a trial. No commitment.
  2. Register with Parks & Rec. Search "Judo" in the online catalog and sign up. Family discounts apply when more than one family member enrolls.
  3. Get a gi when you're ready. Athletic clothes are fine at first. Instructors can point you to affordable options.
Register via Parks & Rec

What to bring

  • Clothing. A judo gi if you have one, otherwise sweatpants and a t-shirt with no zippers, buttons or metal.
  • A water bottle.
  • Flip-flops or slides for the walk between the door and the mat. Shoes never go on the tatami.
  • Trimmed nails, no jewelry, and long hair tied back.

Your first class is just showing up.

Tuesday or Thursday, 5:30 pm. Sweatpants and a t-shirt are all you need. We'll take care of the rest.
