Judo for Kids

Ages 6 and up. A sport that builds confidence, body awareness, and respect — and it's a lot of fun.

Why judo for your child?

Judo is one of the most popular youth sports in the world, and for good reason. It's an Olympic sport with no punching or kicking — the goal is to throw, pin, and control using balance and leverage. That makes it a great fit for kids of every size and temperament.

Our assistant head instructor, Sensei Davin Tillman, started at DeLeon as a kid — he began here in 1992 at age 5. We know what it means to grow up in this dojo.

Kids learn to:

  • Fall safely — the first and most useful skill in judo, on and off the mat.
  • Respect partners, instructors, and the traditions of the dojo (bowing, etiquette, Japanese terms).
  • Persist — belts are earned through steady effort, not bought.
  • Be part of a team in a club where older students look out for younger ones.

Your child's first class

  1. Arrive a few minutes early

    Wear sweatpants and a t-shirt (no zippers or metal), bring water, and bring flip-flops for walking to the mat. Nails trimmed, no jewelry.

  2. Bow in and warm up

    Class starts with a bow and a group warm-up. New students then work with an instructor on ukemi — how to fall without getting hurt.

  3. Learn a first technique

    Most kids learn their first throw and pin on day one, practiced slowly and cooperatively with a partner.

  4. Decide if it's a fit

    If your child enjoys it, register through Petaluma Parks & Rec. We'll help you find a gi.

Safety

Judo is a contact sport, but safety is built into how it's taught. Students learn to fall before they learn to throw. Techniques are practiced cooperatively, and free practice (randori) is introduced only when a student is ready. Choking and arm-locking techniques are not taught to young children. Every class is supervised by certified black-belt instructors with decades of experience teaching kids.
