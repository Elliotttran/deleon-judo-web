About DeLeon Judo Club

A volunteer-run, non-profit dojo serving Petaluma since January 1970.

Our history

DeLeon Judo Club was founded in January 1970 by Jim Santiago DeLeon. For more than five decades the club has taught judo to generations of Petaluma families — many of today's instructors first stepped on the mat here as children.

Since 2007 the club has been led by Sensei Henry Kaku, a Kodokan black belt with over 50 years in judo. The club operates as a non-profit in partnership with the City of Petaluma Parks & Recreation Department, which handles all registration.

Our philosophy

Our goal is to impart to our students not only the physical skills of judo, but its philosophy and traditions. Judo — "The Gentle Way" — was developed by Dr. Jigoro Kano and makes use of leverage, balance, movement, and the opponent's own strength.

Its two core principles guide everything we do:

  • Maximum Efficiency — use the least physical strength necessary, through proper technique and timing.
  • Mutual Welfare and Benefit — the discipline of judo extends beyond the dojo into daily life, and we look out for each other.

We introduce students to judo as a lifetime pursuit, a skilled art requiring infinite perfecting. We keep traditional etiquette and the Japanese names of techniques to honor the art's heritage. A judo dojo is a place of stability and community, where people watch out for you, in and out of the dojo.

Our affiliations

We are not an independent school teaching our own system. DeLeon Judo Club teaches Kodokan judo and belongs to the national organizations that govern it in the United States.

What this means for you: the rank you earn here is certified and portable — it is recognized at any judo club in the country and by clubs abroad. Your instructors are ranked and certified through the same system, and students who want to compete can enter sanctioned tournaments from the local level up to the national championships. See what we teach and how promotion works.

Membership in a national organization is separate from your Parks & Rec class registration. Instructors will let you know when and how to sign up — it is generally required before a promotion or a tournament, not before your first class.

Our yudanshakai: CENCO

Every USJF club belongs to a regional black belt association. Ours is the Central Coast Judo Association — and DeLeon instructors help lead it.

Those two seats matter for our students. The Board of Examiners is the body that sets and oversees rank standards across CENCO's member clubs, and the Board of Managers runs the association's tournaments, clinics, and business. Our students learn the same standard they will be examined against, from the people who help set it — and they train and compete alongside the other clubs in the association.
